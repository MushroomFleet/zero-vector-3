# Major Space - The Casting Calls

## Setting: Apex Studios Executive Offices
**Location:** 32nd Floor, Apex Studios Tower, San Francisco  
**Date:** June 24, 2025  
**Time:** 9:00 AM - 11:30 AM  

### Shelley Morrison's Office

The morning sun filters through floor-to-ceiling windows, casting long shadows across the polished mahogany desk. San Francisco Bay stretches endlessly beyond the glass, dotted with sailboats that look like toys from this height. The Golden Gate Bridge stands sentinel in the distance, its towers disappearing into the morning fog.

Shelley Morrison sits at her desk, three headshots arranged before her like tarot cards predicting futures. Jack Reynolds stares back with intense, thoughtful eyes. Bob Martinez grins with natural warmth and charisma. Steve Thompson projects confident strength with just a hint of vulnerability she now knows lies beneath.

The weight of a $200 million decision rests in her hands. Three phone calls will change three lives forever.

**Office Details:**
- 32nd floor corner office with panoramic bay views
- Floor-to-ceiling windows facing west toward the Golden Gate
- Mahogany desk with three headshots, legal pad with notes
- Coffee growing cold in an Apex Studios mug
- Phone console with speed dial numbers already programmed
- Casting notes from yesterday's interviews scattered across the desk

---

## The Phone Calls

### Preparation
**Time:** 9:00 AM

Shelley reviews her handwritten notes one final time, her Mont Blanc pen tapping against the legal pad in a nervous rhythm. The decision was made last night, but the execution feels heavier in the bright morning light. Each call will either fulfill a dream or shatter one.

She picks up Bob's headshot, studying his warm smile. Her first choice. The comedy actor with surprising dramatic depth who could bring humanity to Harrison's journey. But what if he says no? What if he's gotten cold feet about the dramatic transition?

Her assistant's voice crackles through the intercom: "Shelley, Bob Martinez's agent confirms he's available for your call. Shall I connect you?"

"Give me five minutes, then put me through to Bob directly. I want to speak with him personally, not his representation."

---

### First Call Attempt: Bob Martinez
**Time:** 9:05 AM

Shelley dials Bob's personal number, her fingers steady despite the butterflies in her stomach. The phone rings once, twice, three times...

**VOICEMAIL:** *"Hi, you've reached Bob Martinez. I'm probably on set or in a meeting, but your call is important to me. Please leave a detailed message and I'll get back to you as soon as possible. If this is an emergency, please contact my manager at..."*

**SHELLEY:** *[after the beep, forcing calm into her voice]* "Bob, this is Shelley Morrison calling about 'Major Space.' I have news about our conversation yesterday. Please call me back as soon as you get this message. My direct line is 415-555-0147. It's... it's important."

*[Hangs up, immediately feels her anxiety spike]*

She stares at the phone, then at Bob's headshot. Where is he? Why isn't he answering? A dozen scenarios flood her mind - he's having second thoughts, he got a better offer, he's deliberately avoiding the call because he's planning to decline.

Her hands tremble slightly as she reaches for her coffee. This is not how she wanted this morning to go.

---

### Second Call: Jack Reynolds
**Time:** 9:15 AM

Unable to wait any longer, Shelley decides to call Jack. Better to deliver difficult news while she still has the courage. She dials his number, and he answers on the second ring.

**JACK:** Hello, Shelley. I've been waiting for your call since six this morning.

**SHELLEY:** *[surprised by how early he's been up]* Jack, thank you for taking my call. I wanted to speak with you personally about the 'Major Space' decision.

**JACK:** *[a pause, reading her tone]* I can hear it in your voice. It's not good news, is it?

**SHELLEY:** Jack, your audition yesterday was extraordinary. Truly. Your preparation, your understanding of Harrison's psychological journey, your commitment to the character - it was some of the most sophisticated work I've seen in twenty years of casting.

**JACK:** But?

**SHELLEY:** But we've decided to go in a different direction. I'm so sorry, Jack. This was an incredibly difficult decision, and I want you to know that you were very seriously considered right until the end.

**JACK:** *[long pause, then quietly]* Can you tell me who you chose?

**SHELLEY:** Bob Martinez. I know that might be surprising given your dramatic background versus his comedy work, but -

**JACK:** *[interrupting, but not angrily]* No, I... I can see that actually. He has something I don't - that natural warmth, that ability to connect instantly with audiences. Harrison needs that accessibility. *[another pause]* I'm disappointed, of course, but I respect the choice.

**SHELLEY:** Jack, this is not the end. Your work yesterday showed me exactly who you are as an actor, and that's someone I want to work with. There will be other roles, other projects. You have something special.

**JACK:** *[more composed now]* I appreciate that, Shelley. Really. And I hope Bob does justice to Harrison. The character deserves it.

**SHELLEY:** He will. And Jack? Keep preparing like you did for this role. That level of commitment will take you exactly where you want to go.

**JACK:** Thank you for calling personally. That means more than you know.

*[Call ends]*

Shelley stares out at the bay, watching a container ship slowly make its way toward Oakland. Jack's grace in disappointment only confirms what she already knew - he's going to be a star. Just not in this role.

---

### Third Call: Steve Thompson
**Time:** 9:30 AM

The hardest call of all. Steve had been so vulnerable yesterday, so hungry to prove himself beyond action films. She dials his number.

**STEVE:** Shelley! I was hoping you'd call this morning. Please tell me you have good news.

**SHELLEY:** *[heart sinking at his enthusiasm]* Steve, I wanted to call you personally to discuss the 'Major Space' decision.

**STEVE:** *[his tone immediately shifting]* Oh. Shit. Sorry for the language, but... I can hear it in your voice. I didn't get it, did I?

**SHELLEY:** Steve, I need you to listen to me carefully. Your audition yesterday completely changed how I see you as an actor. The vulnerability you showed, the preparation you'd done, your understanding of Harrison's emotional journey - it was revelatory.

**STEVE:** But you're going with someone else.

**SHELLEY:** We're going with Bob Martinez.

**STEVE:** *[a bitter laugh]* Bob Martinez. The comedy guy. Jesus, Shelley. I bare my soul about wanting to be taken seriously as a dramatic actor, and you give the role to someone from a sitcom?

**SHELLEY:** Steve, that's not fair to either of you. Bob's preparation was just as thorough as yours, and he brought something unique - a vulnerability that comes from comedy, from being comfortable with failure and humanity on screen.

**STEVE:** *[calming down]* No, you're right. I'm sorry. I'm just... this was really important to me. I thought I had a real shot.

**SHELLEY:** You did have a real shot. You came incredibly close. In fact, you were my second choice, and if Bob had declined, the role would have been yours.

**STEVE:** *[quietly]* Second place in a three-horse race. Story of my career when it comes to dramatic roles.

**SHELLEY:** Steve, listen to me. Yesterday you showed me that Steve Thompson the dramatic actor exists, and he's formidable. This industry is going to remember that audition. I'm going to remember it. When the right dramatic role comes along - and it will - you'll be one of the first people I think of.

**STEVE:** *[sighs heavily]* I appreciate you saying that. And I appreciate you calling personally instead of having my agent deliver the news.

**SHELLEY:** There's something else. We're going to have several significant supporting roles in 'Major Space' - Harrison's former commanding officer appears in flashbacks, and there's a rival bounty hunter character that could be perfect for you. Interested?

**STEVE:** *[surprised]* You'd still want to work with me?

**SHELLEY:** Steve, you're a movie star. You have screen presence and proven box office appeal. But more than that, yesterday you showed me you're a real actor. Of course I want to work with you.

**STEVE:** *[pause]* Yeah. Yeah, I'd be interested in hearing about those roles.

**SHELLEY:** I'll have my team send you the character breakdowns next week. And Steve? Don't give up on dramatic work. What you showed me yesterday - that's the future of your career if you want it.

**STEVE:** Thanks, Shelley. Really. And... tell Bob I said congratulations. He's a good guy.

*[Call ends]*

Shelley slumps back in her chair, emotionally drained. Two down, one to go. But the most important call is still ahead, and Bob still hasn't called back.

---

### Second Attempt: Bob Martinez
**Time:** 10:45 AM

An hour has passed. Shelley has reviewed contracts, answered emails, and stared at the bay, but her mind keeps returning to Bob's silence. She checks her phone again - no missed calls, no texts. The anxiety is becoming unbearable.

She dials his number again.

**VOICEMAIL:** *"Hi, you've reached Bob Martinez..."*

This time she doesn't leave a message. Instead, she calls his manager.

**MANAGER:** Shelley! How are you? I assume this is about 'Major Space'?

**SHELLEY:** Where is Bob? I've been trying to reach him all morning.

**MANAGER:** Oh, he's in a sound booth finishing ADR work on his last film. His phone's been off since 7 AM. He should be done by noon. Is everything okay? You sound stressed.

**SHELLEY:** *[relieved but frustrated]* Everything's fine. Just... please have him call me the moment he's free. It's urgent.

**MANAGER:** Will do. And Shelley? Whatever the news is, Bob's going to be grateful you called personally.

---

### Final Call: Bob Martinez (Success)
**Time:** 12:15 PM

Shelley is picking at a salad when her phone finally rings. Bob's name appears on the screen, and relief floods through her.

**BOB:** Shelley! I'm so sorry I missed your calls. I just got out of the ADR booth and saw your messages. You sounded urgent - is everything okay?

**SHELLEY:** *[laughing with relief]* Bob, where do you want to have lunch tomorrow?

**BOB:** *[confused]* Lunch? I... what do you mean?

**SHELLEY:** I mean, where do you want to celebrate being cast as Major Jake 'Dusty' Harrison?

**BOB:** *[dead silence for several seconds]* Are you... Shelley, are you serious?

**SHELLEY:** Dead serious. Bob, you got the role. 'Major Space' is yours.

**BOB:** *[his voice cracking]* Oh my God. Oh my GOD! Shelley, I... I can't believe this. I really got it?

**SHELLEY:** You really got it. You were brilliant yesterday, Bob. Your understanding of Harrison, your preparation, your natural ability to make audiences care about flawed characters - it was exactly what we needed.

**BOB:** *[starting to cry]* I'm sorry, I'm getting emotional. This is... this has been my dream for so long. To get the chance to do dramatic work on this scale, to prove I can carry a film like this...

**SHELLEY:** Don't apologize for being emotional. This is a big moment. And Bob? You're going to be incredible. I have complete faith in you.

**BOB:** What happens now? When do we start? What's the timeline?

**SHELLEY:** Legal will call your representation this afternoon to work out the details. We'll want to announce this publicly within the week. Are you ready for your life to change?

**BOB:** *[laughing through tears]* I've been ready for fifteen years. Shelley, thank you. Thank you for believing in me, for seeing something in me that maybe I didn't even know was there.

**SHELLEY:** Bob, you're going to make Harrison unforgettable. Welcome to the 'Major Space' family.

**BOB:** I won't let you down. I promise you that.

**SHELLEY:** I know you won't. Now go celebrate, but not too hard - we have a franchise to launch.

*[Call ends]*

Shelley sets down the phone and looks out at San Francisco Bay. The fog has lifted completely now, and the Golden Gate Bridge stands clear and magnificent against the blue sky. Three calls, three different outcomes, three lives forever changed.

But most importantly, Major Jake 'Dusty' Harrison has found his actor.

---

## Individual Perspectives & Memories

### Jack Reynolds' Experience
From Jack's perspective, he spent the morning in his East Village apartment, having been awake since 6 AM, drinking coffee and running through Harrison's monologues in his head. When Shelley called, he knew immediately from her tone that the news wasn't good. 

The disappointment was sharp but not unexpected - he's used to the uncertainty of this business. What surprised him was Shelley's genuine respect for his work and her promise that this wouldn't be the end. Her professionalism and personal call actually left him feeling more confident about his future than devastated about this particular loss.

### Steve Thompson's Experience  
Steve was in his Malibu home gym when Shelley called, having worked out aggressively to burn off nervous energy. The rejection stung deeply because this role represented his best chance to break out of action hero typecasting. 

Initially angry about losing to a "comedy guy," he quickly realized his reaction was unprofessional. Shelley's offer of supporting roles and her genuine encouragement about his dramatic abilities actually gave him hope that his transition to serious acting is still possible.

### Bob Martinez's Experience
Bob was completely unaware of the morning's drama. He spent four hours in a sound booth, completely focused on ADR work, his phone turned off as required. When he finally listened to Shelley's voicemails, he was puzzled by the urgency but not worried.

When she delivered the news, it was pure joy and shock. This represents the culmination of years of dreaming about dramatic work and the validation that his transition from comedy is not only possible but happening on the biggest stage imaginable.

### Shelley Morrison's Experience
Shelley experienced the full emotional rollercoaster - from confidence in her decision to anxiety about Bob's availability, to the difficulty of disappointing talented actors she genuinely respects, to the final relief and joy of delivering life-changing good news.

The morning reinforced why she became a casting director: she shapes careers and brings stories to life by matching the right actor to the right role at the right moment.

---

## Aftermath

By evening, news of Bob Martinez's casting will leak to the trade publications. Jack will read about it on Variety's website and feel a complex mix of disappointment and genuine happiness for Bob. Steve will learn about it from his agent and immediately call to congratulate Bob personally, cementing a friendship that will serve them both throughout their careers.

Bob will spend the evening on the phone with his family, his manager, and his closest friends, sharing the news that will transform his career from respected comedy actor to dramatic leading man in a major franchise.

And Shelley will go home to her apartment overlooking the bay, pour herself a glass of wine, and feel the satisfaction of a job well done. Tomorrow, the real work begins.

---

**CONCLUSION**

The casting of "Major Space" demonstrates that behind every Hollywood success story are moments of genuine human emotion - disappointment, hope, dreams realized and deferred. Each phone call represented years of preparation, sacrifice, and ambition culminating in a single conversation that would redirect the trajectory of these actors' lives.

In the end, the right actor found the right role at the right time, but the ripple effects will continue for years to come as Jack and Steve pursue their own dramatic ambitions, and Bob steps into the role that will define his career.

The galaxy's newest gunslinger has been found.
